Before the first call

How I read the situation

I had one hour before the founder call. Three things stood out, so that is where the hour went.

The request breaks the room

300 guests can't fit a 260-person venue. If 260 is total occupancy, 250 guests plus speakers, sponsor teams and crew may already be over the limit. Catering locks in 14 days.

AV is the critical gap

$22,000 is the largest unbooked line. No reply in 6 days, and Thanksgiving week cuts our real runway to about 4 weeks. Stage, floor plan and keynote timing all depend on it.

There is almost no cushion

$3,000 contingency is about 2% of budget. The founder's asks cost around $10,500. New spend needs a funding source, and the unsigned fourth sponsor is the obvious one.

First hour: read the signed contracts (venue capacity and overtime clauses, catering change terms), built the headcount sheet, called AV and asked for backup quotes, then prepared two options for the founder.

Rules I run the account by

  • Anything above $5,000 goes to the founder with two options and a recommendation.
  • Contingency is not spent without founder OK.

  • Founder hears bad news from me first, with a plan.

Part 2.1

Reply to the founder's request

Protect the room, the budget and the relationship at the same time.

To: FounderRe: VIP dinner, guest list, keynote

Hi Anna, love this. A VIP dinner after a 6pm keynote makes night one feel special.

One hard limit: the venue holds 260 people in total, so 300 isn't possible in this room. We hold at 250 with a waitlist while I confirm whether crew counts.

Option A: We fund it. VIP dinner for 40 in a private room nearby (~$8,500) and keynote at 6–7pm, dinner at 7:45pm (~$2,000 overtime). About $10,500 extra, new total $150,500. Trade-off: everything you asked for, at a higher spend.

Option B: A sponsor funds it. Same plan, but we offer the dinner to our fourth sponsor as their headline package, which also gets their contract signed. Overtime comes from contingency. Close to $0 extra for you. Trade-off: it depends on them signing by Nov 13. If they pass, we fall back to A.

My recommendation: Option B. It turns a verbal yes into a signed sponsor and protects your budget.

What I need by Thursday: A or B, and your OK to pitch the sponsor. Then everything is confirmed by Friday.

Malena

Part 2.2

The sponsor complaint

1. Within the hour, reply-all (founder copied): "Thanks for flagging this. You'll have a solution today. I'll call you at 3pm." No debate by email.

2. Check the facts: contract and deck vs. floor plan. Then the Production Lead, who sold the deck, and the venue, to find two workable fixes.

3. Call, agree a fix, confirm it in writing within 24 hours, update the floor plan and send the founder a two-line close-out.

Who first: the sponsor, to stop the escalation. Then the Production Lead.

On the call: "You were promised that spot, and it's on us to make it right. Here are two ways we can do that this week."

Part 2.3

The headliner cancels, 48 hours out

1. Call the headliner's agent (15 minutes): is it final? Could they join by video or send a recorded talk? Check refund terms.

2. Call the founder within the hour, before it leaks: "[Headliner] cancelled. I'm on it. Two options: you host a fireside with our strongest speaker, or a backup from our shortlist. You'll have a confirmed name by noon tomorrow."

3. Lock the replacement; update run of show, AV and signage.

Sponsors: I call both personally before any announcement. I offer an on-stage moment in the new headline session and extra visibility, then confirm in writing.

Who first: the agent, for facts. Then the founder.

Part 3

AI workflow: Daily Pulse

The one I would actually use. Every morning it reads new emails, messages (Slack, WhatsApp, iMessage) and Granola meeting notes, plus the hub tracker. It flags blockers, overdue items and unapproved spend, and sends me a daily digest. On Mondays and Thursdays it also drafts the 3-line founder update, and on Fridays the Production Lead digest. Nobody has to chase me, and I don't have to chase anyone.

Gmail · Slack · WhatsApp · Granola→Founders Summit HQ→Claude + instructions→Flags, daily digest, drafts→Malena reviews and edits→Sent

Tool and where it lives

Claude, set up as a scheduled task every morning at 8am (and on demand). It reads Gmail, messages from Slack, WhatsApp and iMessage, and Granola meeting notes (only threads and meetings tagged Founders Summit), plus the hub. It writes new items to the hub's inbox feed, sends my digest by Slack DM, and saves client drafts in Gmail. The hub computes its own flags for overdue items and spend above $5,000, so dates and thresholds are checked twice. For this test the sync is shown, not switched on, because the scenario has no real inbox.

What it can and can't do

  • Can: read sources, add items to the hub feed, update last-contact dates, flag, draft, send my own digest.
  • Can't: send anything to the client, sponsors or vendors, change status, money or dates on its own, approve spend or promise dates. Those changes are proposed to me first.
  • Human check: I read and edit every draft before it goes out. The founder message always gets my own voice.
